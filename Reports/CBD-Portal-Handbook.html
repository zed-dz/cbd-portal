CBD Plant & Labour · Operations Portal

The portal, explained page by page

Everything the portal does, what each button actually means, and the exact steps for the jobs you'll do every week. Written so you can open it mid-task, find the one thing you need, and get back to work.

cbd-portal-gray.vercel.app Same build runs MRA + Hecate Last verified 21 Aug 2026
Start

Read this first

The portal runs the whole labour-hire operation in one place: who works for you, where they're going, what they did, what they get paid, and what the client gets billed. It replaced the paper-and-spreadsheet version of all of that.

It has two faces

Admin portal
What office staff see. The full sidebar — workers, allocations, timesheets, payroll, clients, settings.
Worker portal
What a worker sees on their phone. Their own jobs, their own timesheets, their tickets. Nothing else.

To see exactly what a worker sees, hit 👁 Worker View in the top bar. You're previewing as yourself. ✕ Exit Preview brings you back. Use this before you send anything out — it saves the "what does this look like on their end?" round trip.

Who can do what

RoleGets
AdminEverything. Workers, money, settings, the lot.
ManagerSame as admin. For senior office staff.
WorkerThe phone portal only. Their own jobs and timesheets.

People sign themselves up on the login page. The very first person to ever sign up becomes admin automatically. Everyone after that lands as a worker, and an admin promotes them from the Workers page.

Your first ten minutes

  1. Open the portal and sign in. If you've never logged in, use the Create account tab.
  2. Land on Dashboard. Read the six number cards top to bottom — that's the state of the business right now.
  3. Click 👷 Workers. Find yourself. Check your name, email and role are right.
  4. Click 🕐 Timesheets. This is where the week's work arrives. Anything red in the sidebar badge is waiting on a human.
  5. Hit 👁 Worker View in the top bar, look around, then ✕ Exit Preview. Now you know both sides.
The badges tell you what needs you

Those little coloured numbers in the sidebar aren't decoration — each one is a live count of things sitting in a queue. red on Timesheets means timesheets are pending. yellow on Pending Workers means people can't log in yet. If every badge is quiet, there's nothing waiting on you.

What the words mean

The portal uses a handful of terms in a very specific way. Get these five right and the rest of the system reads itself.

TermWhat it actually means
Pay hours Hours the worker gets paid for. Not always the hours they stood on site — a rained-off day pays 8 even if they went home at 10am.
Charge hours Hours the client gets billed for. On that same rained-off day this might be 4, or 0. Pay and charge are deliberately separate numbers.
Scenario What kind of day it was. Standard, rain-off, leave, training, public holiday. The scenario is what decides the pay/charge split above.
A / B / C The three price tiers on a client's rate card. A = normal, B = 1.5×, C = 2×. These are charge rates — what you bill.
RDO Rostered day off. A full-timer working a normal weekday banks 0.4h per day instead of being paid it. RDO hours are not paid in that run — they're paid when the day is taken.
Allocation A booking. "This worker goes to that client's site on these dates." Made before the work happens.
Timesheet The record of what actually happened. Made after the work happens.
Schedule of Rates A client's agreed price list, line by line — Labourer, 8t Excavator, Tipping Fee — each with its own unit and A/B/C prices.
Wet hire Machine supplied with an operator. Tracked separately on the daily timesheet.
Take 5 The pre-start safety check a worker fills in on their phone: what's the task, what could hurt me, what am I doing about it.

👷 Workers

For
Everyone who works for you — full-time, casual and subcontractor.
Open it
Hiring someone, changing a pay rate, checking a ticket, offboarding.
Watch for
Pay rates here feed payroll directly. A wrong number here becomes a wrong payslip.

Adding a worker

Hit + Add Worker. You must fill in:

  • Full name — this must match their name in Xero exactly, or the payroll push will skip them.
  • Email — this is their login.
  • Worker Type — Full-Time, Casual or Subcontractor. This changes how they get paid, so get it right.
  • Pay Rate Regular ($/hr) — the base rate everything else is calculated from.

Worth adding: mobile, address, job title, OT rate, and licences. Subcontractors get an extra ABN field.

The three worker types are not cosmetic

Full-time — banks RDO hours, and gets topped up to a full 8h day if sent home early on a normal weekday.

Casual — gets a travel allowance every shift they actually work. No RDO.

Subcontractor — paid on their own two rates plus a flat loading for nights and weekends. Not on the award penalty ladder at all, and excluded from the Xero staff payroll push.

Finding people

  • Search by name or email.
  • 🪪 All Licences dropdown — filter to everyone holding a given ticket. Type ewp to find every EWP holder.
  • Filter by worker type or status.

Offboarding — archive, don't delete

When someone leaves, archive them. They vanish from the active roster and the dropdowns, but every timesheet, payslip and record they ever touched stays intact. Deleting would tear a hole in your history.

You pick a reason, and the portal records who archived them and when:

Reason
Resigned (left voluntarily)
End of contract
Let go (performance / conduct)
No-show / lost contact
Medical / injury
Not currently working
Retired
Other

Switch the view between Active, Archived and All. If someone comes back, hit ↺ Restore.

Two licence fields, on purpose

Quick Licences is free text for fast capture and searching. Certifications is the real compliance record with expiry dates, and it's what drives the dashboard alerts. Keep both. They do different jobs.

Share links

Each worker has two private links. Share profile gives a client a read-only card — no pay rates, no address, no email. Onboarding link lets the worker fill in their own details. Both are unguessable keys; treat them like passwords.

How a timesheet gets approved — it's mostly automatic

You usually don't have to do anything. The chain runs itself:

The automatic sign-off chain

  1. The worker submits their daily timesheet from their phone.
  2. The portal immediately emails and texts the site supervisor a private approval link.
  3. The supervisor opens the link and accepts. No login needed.
  4. That acceptance approves the timesheet and makes it billable in one step. No admin action required.
  5. If nobody responds within 7 days, the portal approves it automatically and rings the notification bell so you know it happened.

You still have full override. In the timesheet's View panel you can:

  • Send / Resend to supervisor — if the first one didn't land.
  • Mark accepted (verbal) — the supervisor okayed it on the phone. This also approves it.
  • Adjust or reject at any time, even after approval.
If the bell says "sign-off blocked"

That means the portal couldn't reach a supervisor — usually no site contact on the project, or a bad phone number. The timesheet is stuck until you either fix the contact and resend, or mark it accepted verbally. Don't ignore it; the 7-day auto-approve will eventually pass it through unchecked.

Scenarios — the most important field on the page

A scenario tells the portal what kind of day it was. It sets pay hours and charge hours independently, which is exactly what you want on a rain day.

Adjustments are tracked

If an admin edits a worker's submitted times, the portal keeps the original start, finish and break, and stamps who changed it and when. You'll see it on the edit form, the detail view and the printed timesheet. A worker fixing their own pending sheet doesn't count as an adjustment.

🏗 Clients & Rates

For
Who you supply to, their projects, and what they've agreed to pay.
Open it
New client, new site, or a rate change.
Watch for
Never create a second client record with the same name. See the warning below.
One business = one client record. Always.

Duplicate client records with the same name have already caused real mis-billing here — one "Matt Civil" priced at $100/hr with a full rate card, another at $50/hr with none, and the portal picking whichever it found first. If a client needs multiple sites, add projects underneath them. Never a second client row.

Adding a client

+ Add Client, then:

  • Name — required.
  • Contact — the billing and admin person, not the site supervisor.
  • Default client rates — A, B and C in $/hr. These are the fallback, used when nothing on the Schedule of Rates matches the role on a timesheet.
  • Email domains — e.g. sydneywater.com.au. Any email from that domain auto-tags to this client in the Inbox. Consumer domains like gmail.com are ignored on purpose.

Projects (the job sites)

Click Projects on a client row. Each project carries its own:

  • Name, site, address, start and end date, status
  • Site contact — name, email, phone. This is the person who gets the timesheet approval link. A client with three sites can have three different site contacts.
  • Required roles, and notes

Schedule of Rates

This is the client's agreed price list, line by line. Each row is one billable item — Labourer, Confined Space Entry, 8t Excavator, Tipping Fee, Travel per km — with its own unit (hour, shift, day, ton, m³, m², lm, each, km, unit) and its own A/B/C prices. Items group on screen by category: labour, plant, attachments, materials, allowances, other.

Three ways to get rates in:

+ Add line item
One row at a time. Fine for a single addition.
+ Add many
A grid for typing 3–10 rows in one go.
📤 Upload rates
Paste a whole rate card from a PDF or Excel, or upload a CSV. This is the one you want.

The uploader works out the delimiter itself, matches your column headers however they're spelled, normalises units (Hour, hr, H all become hour), and treats POR, POA, TBA and blanks as "no price". You get a live preview of exactly what will be saved — and what got skipped — before anything is committed.

Role names have to match, near enough

When a timesheet is priced, the portal matches the role on the timesheet to a line on the rate card. It's forgiving — it ignores punctuation, treats "Labourer" and "Labour" as the same, and will match "Operator - Dozer" to a line called just "Operator".

But if it finds nothing at all, it quietly falls back to the client's default A/B/C, and the Payroll page marks that row fallback. Treat a fallback tag as a job to do — it means you're billing a guess, not the agreed price.

Common Roles

A shared library of role names — Excavator Operator, Dogman, Skilled Labourer, Foreman. It only powers the type-ahead suggestions when you're writing a rate card or a project. Adding one here makes it suggestible everywhere, immediately.

Running a pay period

  1. Set Pay period from and to. Nothing can be pushed to Xero until you do.
  2. Narrow down if you want — by worker type, or by Worker to check one person.
  3. Read the tiles: normal hours, OT 1.5×, OT 2×, RDO, total pay, total charge — each with its dollars.
  4. Scan the Pay summary by worker table at the bottom. One line per person, ending in what they're owed. This is the sheet to check before you pay anyone.
  5. Click Select Unexported to pick every approved timesheet not yet sent.
  6. ↑ Push to Xero, or export a CSV if you'd rather import by hand.

fallback — no rate card line matched this role, so it's billing the client's default A/B/C. Add the missing line on the client's Schedule of Rates.

Why a push gets skipped

Xero matches on first and last name, exactly. If the push reports "skipped", it's almost always that the worker's name doesn't match their Xero record, or they're not assigned to a payroll calendar in Xero. Subcontractors are skipped by design.

The rules

How pay is worked out

Every shift gets split into three buckets — normal time, 1.5× and 2× — and each bucket is paid at its own rate. The split happens once, when the timesheet is saved, so what's printed on the timesheet and what's on the payslip can never disagree.

The shiftHow the hours split
Sunday · Public Holiday · Saturday nightEvery hour at 2×
Saturday dayFirst 2h at 1.5×, everything after at 2×
Weekday nightFirst 8h at 1.5×, everything after at 2×
Weekday day — full-time7.6h normal + 0.4h banked to RDO, then 1.5× to the 10th hour, then 2×
Weekday day — casual / subbie8h normal, then 1.5× to the 10th hour, then 2×
Leave, rain-off, training, LWOPAll ordinary. Penalties never apply
The Saturday rule catches people out

Saturday day is only 1.5× for the first two hours — then it's double time. Saturday night is double time from the first minute. These are different rules, and it's the most common thing to get wrong from memory.

Allowances

AllowanceAmountWhen
Travel$15.00Casuals, per shift actually worked. Not on leave, rain-off or training days.
Meal$18.70Automatic once a shift hits 9.5 hours.
Geo loading+10%Manual tick box. Multiplies the base pay.
Subbie night/weekend$10.00Flat add-on for subcontractors on nights or weekends.

Full-time minimum day

A full-timer sent home early on a normal weekday is still paid a full 8 hours, while the client is only charged the hours actually worked. Two 4-hour shifts on one day already make the 8, so no top-up applies. It doesn't apply to weekends, public holidays, or any non-standard scenario.

Charging is a different set of rules from paying. Same shift, different maths. This is deliberate, and it's the thing most worth understanding on this page.

Which price it uses

The portal looks for a price in this order, and stops at the first one it finds:

  1. The Schedule of Rates line matching the role on the timesheet — the agreed price.
  2. The client's default A/B/C — flagged fallback on the Payroll page.
  3. The client's old single-rate columns — flagged ⚠ no rates.
Known gap — pay can exceed charge

Pay reaches 2× in places charging never does. A Saturday day shift is paid at double time from hour three, but billed at B for its whole length. Weekday overtime bills at B however long it runs and never reaches C.

This has not been confirmed as intended. If you're quoting a long Saturday or a heavy overtime week, check the margin by hand before you commit to a price.

Job recipes

The things you'll actually do, start to finish.

Send a worker to a job tomorrow

  1. Workers — check they exist, are active, and their tickets aren't expired.
  2. Allocations → + Create Allocation.
  3. Fill in worker, client, project, site, dates and arrival time.
  4. Fill in the site supervisor and phone. Without it, their timesheet can't be signed off.
  5. Set status to Confirmed and save.
  6. Read any double-booking warning before you override it.

Approve a rain day

  1. Timesheets → filter to Pending.
  2. Open the row.
  3. Set the scenario: Rain-Off Cancelled if they were sent straight home, Rain-Off Partial if they got about half a day in.
  4. Pay and charge hours fill in automatically — 8 paid, 0 or 4 charged.
  5. Set status to Approved and save.

Run last week's payroll

  1. Payroll → set the pay period dates.
  2. Check the Pay summary by worker table at the bottom. Does each person's total look right?
  3. Look for fallback or ⚠ no rates tags and fix the rate cards behind them.
  4. Export Pay Breakdown CSV if you want a copy to check off screen.
  5. Select Unexported.
  6. ↑ Push to Xero — or export the Xero CSV and import it by hand.

Check exactly what one person is owed

  1. Payroll → set the date range.
  2. Pick them in the Worker dropdown.
  3. Read the Normal / OT 1.5× / OT 2× columns. Hover any cell to see the rate behind it.
  4. The Pay summary table at the bottom now shows just them, ending in their total.

Onboard a new client with their price list

  1. Clients & Rates → + Add Client. Check first that they don't already exist.
  2. Add contact details, default A/B/C rates, and their email domain.
  3. Add their Projects, each with its own site contact.
  4. Open Schedule of Rates → 📤 Upload rates.
  5. Paste the rate card straight from their PDF or Excel.
  6. Read the preview. Fix anything it skipped, then save.

Get a new worker onto the app

  1. Workers → + Add Worker. Name must match Xero exactly.
  2. Save. They now exist, but can't log in.
  3. Pending Workers → find them → send the invite.
  4. They open the link, complete their profile, and create their account.
  5. Tell them to add it to their home screen.
  6. Licence Agent → add their tickets and expiry dates.

Find everyone with a particular ticket

  1. Workers → open the 🪪 All Licences dropdown.
  2. Pick the licence — or type part of it, like ewp.
  3. The list filters to only those workers.

When it goes wrong

What you're seeingWhat to do
A worker can't log in Pending Workers → check their app status → resend the invite. A worker record alone doesn't grant access.
Xero push says "skipped" The name doesn't match Xero exactly, or they're not on a payroll calendar in Xero. Subcontractors are skipped on purpose.
Xero connection lost Payroll → Reconnect Xero.
A pay rate looks wrong Check the worker's base rate in Workers, then check the date. Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays and night shifts all pay differently. See How pay is worked out.
Approved timesheet isn't in Payroll It needs approval and supervisor sign-off. Check the timesheet's View panel. If it's already been pushed it'll show ✓ sent.
A charge amount looks wrong Look for the fallback tag. It means no rate card line matched that role, so it's billing a default. Add the missing line.
Bell says "sign-off blocked" No reachable site supervisor on that project. Add the contact and resend, or mark it accepted verbally.
New emails aren't showing in the Inbox The sender must be a known worker or client, or on a client's domain. Add them, then hit ↻ Sync.
Every Inbox thread shows the wrong name Someone added the team email address itself as a worker. Delete that worker row, then disconnect and reconnect the team email.
Same client appears twice Stop and get it merged before invoicing. Duplicate clients have caused real mis-billing. Don't just pick one.
Need to change an overtime threshold Payroll Config. Change one value at a time, and check the next pay run carefully.
